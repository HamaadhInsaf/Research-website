References and Further Reading

Source List

References

Sources are listed alphabetically in APA 7th edition style. Letters after a year (for example 2020a and 2020b) separate two reports by the same author in the same year. In-text, ERO means the Education Review Office.

01

Education Gazette. (2020, April 15). Rolling out distance learning across Aotearoa. Ministry of Education. https://gazette.education.govt.nz/articles/rolling-out-distance-learning-across-aotearoa

Used for: Home Learning TV, Māori-medium programmes and printed packs.

02

Education Review Office. (2020a). COVID-19: Impact on schools and early childhood services – Interim report. https://ero.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2021-04/Covid-19%20Impact%20on%20schools%20and%20early%20childhood%20services%20interim%20report.pdf

Used for: School leaders’ choices: wellbeing, digital practices and whānau communication.

03

Education Review Office. (2020b). COVID-19: Learning in lockdown. https://ero.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2021-03/Covid%2019%20Learning%20in%20Lockdown.pdf

Used for: Survey of 10,106 students and 694 teachers: engagement, workload, access and wellbeing.

04

Education Review Office. (2021a). The impact of COVID-19 on schools. https://www.evidence.ero.govt.nz/documents/the-impact-of-covid-19-on-schools-january-2021/

Used for: Student safety, progress concerns and deferred assessment after lockdown.

05

Education Review Office. (2021b). The impact of COVID-19 on teachers and principals. https://www.evidence.ero.govt.nz/documents/the-impact-of-covid-19-on-teachers-and-principals-december-2021

Used for: Teacher and principal workload, wellbeing and lasting digital change.

06

Erlam, G. D., Garrett, N., Gasteiger, N., Lau, K., Hoare, K., Agarwal, S., & Haxell, A. (2021). What really matters: Experiences of emergency remote teaching in university teaching and learning during the COVID-19 pandemic. Frontiers in Education, 6, Article 639842. https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2021.639842

Used for: Peer-reviewed survey of academics at an Auckland university.

07

Fletcher, J., Klopsch, B., Everatt, J., & Sliwka, A. (2022). Preparing student teachers post-pandemic: Lessons learnt from principals and teachers in New Zealand and Germany. Educational Review, 74(3), 609–629. https://doi.org/10.1080/00131911.2021.2007053

Used for: Peer-reviewed interviews with 48 teachers and principals.

08

Godber, K. A., & Atkins, D. R. (2021). COVID-19 impacts on teaching and learning: A collaborative autoethnography by two higher education lecturers. Frontiers in Education, 6, Article 647524. https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2021.647524

Used for: Peer-reviewed article on teaching challenges in an Auckland university.

09

Hipkins, C. (2020, April 8). COVID-19: Government moving quickly to roll out learning from home [Press release]. New Zealand Government. https://www.beehive.govt.nz/release/covid19-government-moving-quickly-roll-out-learning-home

Used for: The government’s four-channel learning-from-home package.

10

Hunter, J., Hunter, R., Tupouniua, J., & Leach, G. (2022). Bringing the home into school: Learning and connecting through mathematics education during the time of a pandemic. Educational Studies in Mathematics, 111, 207–224. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10649-022-10157-1

Used for: Peer-reviewed interviews with 20 teachers and school leaders.

11

Joseph, D., & Trinick, R. (2021). ‘Staying apart yet keeping together’: Challenges and opportunities of teaching during COVID-19 across the Tasman. New Zealand Journal of Educational Studies, 56(2), 209–226. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40841-021-00211-6

Used for: Peer-reviewed study of teacher educators in Auckland and Melbourne.

12

Ministry of Education. (2022). Evaluation of provision of connectivity and devices – A COVID-19 response. Education Counts. https://www.educationcounts.govt.nz/publications/schooling2/digital-technology/evaluation-of-provision-of-connectivity-and-devices-a-covid-19-response

Used for: Figures and limits of the device and internet rollout.

13

Ministry of Education. (2025, August 11). Tackling the digital divide during COVID-19. New Zealand Digital Government. https://www.digital.govt.nz/showcase/tackling-the-digital-divide-during-covid-19

Used for: Digital exclusion, affordability and working with iwi and communities.

14

Ritchie, J. (2023). Wellbeing learnings from pandemic pedagogies in Aotearoa New Zealand. New Zealand Journal of Educational Studies, 58(1), 109–132. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40841-023-00278-3

Used for: Peer-reviewed survey of 51 New Zealand teachers on wellbeing and whānau relationships.

15

Rood, C., & Barbour, M. (2024). Virtual learning for Māori students: Examining culturally responsive pedagogies and equity. Journal of Open, Flexible and Distance Learning, 28(1), 8–27. https://doi.org/10.61468/jofdl.v28i1.643

Used for: Peer-reviewed article on equity and the digital divide for Māori learners.

16

Royal Commission of Inquiry into COVID-19 Lessons Learned. (2024, November 28). Education during the pandemic. https://www.covid19lessons.royalcommission.nz/reports-lessons-learned/experiences-of-the-covid-19-pandemic/education-during-the-pandemic

Used for: What the public told the Commission about closures and distance learning.

17

Thomsen, P. S., Tuiburelevu, L., Keil, M., Leenen-Young, M., Sisifa, S. P., Müller, K., Veukiso-Ulugia, A., Manuela, S., & Naepi, S. (2021). Practising Pacific pedagogies during New Zealand’s level four lockdown: Pacific early career academics and COVID-19. Waikato Journal of Education, 26(1), 149–161. https://doi.org/10.15663/wje.v26i1.754

Used for: Peer-reviewed article on inequities affecting Pacific students.

18

Trafford, J., Haxell, A., Lau, K., Carlson, G., Rebelo da Silva, A. P., Hart, A., De Freitas, T. R., & Rope, S. (2021). Covid-19 (in) a class of its own: Student and teacher musings regarding their learnings and well-being when moving a large blended first year class virtually overnight. Frontiers in Education, 6, Article 639466. https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2021.639466

Used for: Peer-reviewed perspective from a New Zealand university on post-pandemic teaching.

19

Wenmoth, D. (2022). Evidence review: Digital technologies in education during the COVID-19 pandemic. Ministry of Education. https://www.educationcounts.govt.nz/publications/all-schooling/digital-technologies-in-education-during-the-covid-19-pandemic

Used for: Flexible and blended learning, digital skills and teacher workload.

20

Yates, A., & Starkey, L. (2021). Were we ready? New Zealand high school students’ experiences of online learning during school closures of Covid-19, 2020. New Zealand Annual Review of Education, 25, 20–38. https://doi.org/10.26686/nzaroe.v25.6912

Used for: Peer-reviewed survey of 1,975 senior students.
